1. Course Philosophy: What Genomic Medicine Is (and Isn't)

For most of human history, medicine has been an empirical, reactive art: wait for an organ to fail, describe the gross anatomy, and administer blockbuster therapies that—for the top 10 highest-grossing drugs in the US—fail to help between 75% and 90% of the patients who take them.

Genomic medicine re-conceptualizes human pathology as an informational and evolutionary science. DNA and RNA are digital code with physical consequences. By reading the patient's molecular blueprint and tracking somatic evolutionary trajectories in real time, we can replace empirical trial-and-error with targeted molecular mechanisms.

However, this field is also drowning in unvetted commercial hype, dubious algorithmic claims, and direct-to-consumer pseudoscience. In this course, we will not treat genomics or AI as magical black boxes. We question everything. Every paper, test, drug, and predictive model we examine will be subjected to a rigorous 4-step skeptical audit:

🔍 The 4-Step Critical Scaffold
  1. The Mechanism: What is the exact biophysical or molecular disruption?
  2. The Data Rigor: What was the cohort size, ancestry diversity, and actual effect size (not just an inflated p-value)?
  3. Clinical Value: Does this improve patient survival, or is it an expensive surrogate endpoint with zero clinical utility?
  4. The Hype Audit: What did the marketing leave out? What did the LLM hallucinate? Where does the model fail?

2. Classroom Format: The Active 2.5-Hour Workshop

Sitting passively through a 2.5-hour evening lecture produces near-zero retention. This course is run as a high-tempo, modular workshop divided into three distinct operational blocks:

Grades reflect both real-time in-class competence and rigorous written synthesis.

4. Generative AI Policy: The Two-Track Contract

🤖 Instructor AI Disclosure
Transparency is a two-way street. I actively use advanced AI systems as collaborative research, coding, and curriculum-design partners—helping refine lecture narratives, generate interactive clinical sprint datasets, update case studies to 2026 standards, and build our course webapp. AI is a scientific superpower when directed by human domain expertise; it is an embarrassing liability when used as a mindless substitute for thought.

On the title page of your Midterm and Final projects, you must explicitly declare your track:

Track A: AI-Assisted (Graded Against Expert Reviewer Standards)

  • Permitted Scope: You are encouraged to use LLMs (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) for structuring, outlining, iterative drafting, and literature exploration.
  • The Tradeoff: Your submission is evaluated against professional scientific standards. Superficial AI fluff ("Cancer is a complex disease impacting millions worldwide..."), vague generalities, or lack of granular mechanistic detail will be penalized ruthlessly.
  • Mandatory AI Audit Table: You must include a dedicated 1-page section documenting at least two specific instances where the AI produced an error, oversimplification, or hallucination, and provide the verified primary literature correction.
  • Zero-Tolerance Citation Policy: Every cited claim must include an authentic PubMed ID (PMID) or DOI. A single hallucinated citation results in an automatic zero for the bibliography and a 1-letter grade deduction.

Track B: 100% Human-Authored (Traditional Undergrad Standard)

  • Standard: Graded on standard undergraduate rubrics (synthesis, clarity, and biological understanding).
  • The Guardrail: If an unacknowledged Track B paper is flagged for AI-generated text or fabricated citations, it is treated as an intentional academic integrity violation (automatic zero on the assignment).
