1. Executive Summary & Scientific Mission

In the first six weeks of Genomics in Medicine, we explored the primary layers of genomic information: single-nucleotide variants, cytogenetic copy number alterations, polygenic risk scores, 3D chromatin conformation, tissue-specific eQTLs, and host-microbiome interactions.

Your Mission: Select one human disease or syndrome that resonates with you personally, intellectually, or clinically. You will author a publication-grade Multi-Omic Diagnostic & Mechanistic Profile characterizing how these distinct informational tiers converge to cause human pathology, influence penetrance, and guide targeted therapeutics.

This project is structured to cultivate genuine scientific competency. To ensure your synthesis is rooted in authentic data rather than superficial generalities, you will directly retrieve, analyze, and embed figures from primary public databases (UCSC Genome Browser, GTEx Portal, ClinVar, AlphaFold / RCSB PDB, and the GWAS / PGS Catalog).

👥 Collaboration Policy: Solo or Teams of Two

To mirror the collaborative team science of clinical pathology and genomics, you may choose to author this research project individually or in teams of exactly two (2) students:

  • Single Joint Submission: The team submits one single manuscript on Canvas. Either team member may submit the final PDF.
  • Title Page Byline: Clearly display both authors' full names, official Temple AccessNet IDs, student ID numbers, Temple email addresses, and enrollment status (BIOL 3111 Standard, BIOL 3111 Honors, or BIOL 5111 Graduate).
  • Shared Accountability & Grade: Both co-authors receive the identical baseline project score and identical early-bird bonus points based on the Canvas submission timestamp.
  • Author Contribution Statement (CRediT Model): Include a brief section (1 paragraph or bulleted list placed on the title page or immediately before the bibliography) specifying the division of labor across five core tasks: literature synthesis, database coordinate curation, figure generation & annotation, narrative drafting, and forensic citation/AI quality control.
  • Mixed Teams: If a team combines a Standard undergraduate with an Honors or Graduate student, the submission must fulfill the Honors Track specification in full.

2. The Two-Track Generative AI Contract

🤖 Notice of Algorithmic Pre-Screening
Transparency and verification are fundamental to science. All midterm submissions will be screened through an automated computational pipeline prior to human faculty review. This pipeline performs three automated audits:
  1. Live PubMed ID (PMID) Verification: Automated querying of the NCBI E-utilities API to confirm that every single cited PMID exists and discusses the claimed biology.
  2. Genomic Coordinate Validation: Automated checks confirming reported chromosome coordinates, transcript IDs, and dbSNP rsIDs correspond to genuine entries in the GRCh38/hg38 build.
  3. Synthetic Text Profiling: Detection of zero-shot commercial LLM syntactic boilerplate and ungrounded prose.

On your title page, you must explicitly declare your track:

Track A

AI-Assisted Research & Synthesis

Standard: Graded against Expert Peer Reviewer Standards (*Nature Genetics* / *Lancet* rigor).

  • Permitted Tools: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, NotebookLM for outlining, literature search, and drafting.
  • Ruthless Fluff Penalty: Superficial AI introductions or vague hand-waving explanations will be penalized aggressively.
  • Mandatory AI Audit Table (1 Page): Must document ≥2 specific instances where the AI produced an error, coordinate hallucination, or oversimplification, detailing your literature-backed correction.
  • Zero-Tolerance Citation Policy: Every claim requires an authentic PMID/DOI. A single fake citation results in an automatic zero on the bibliography and a 1-letter grade deduction.
Track B

100% Human-Authored

Standard: Graded on standard upper-division undergraduate criteria.

  • Permitted Tools: Traditional literature searches (PubMed, Google Scholar), reference managers (Zotero, Mendeley), and spellcheck.
  • Evaluative Focus: Conceptual clarity, original scientific voice, accurate biological reasoning, and data integration.
  • Integrity Guardrail: If an unacknowledged Track B submission is flagged by the screening pipeline for synthetic AI text or fabricated citations, it is treated as an intentional academic integrity violation (automatic zero on assignment).

4. The Multi-Omic Characterization Tiers (Tiers 1–8 + Tier 9 for Honors)

Your paper must address the core tiers (Tiers 1–8 for all students; Tiers 1–9 for Honors/Graduate students) in distinct, clearly titled narrative sections:

Tier 1: Clinical Phenotype & Diagnostic Scaffolding Macro Perspective

Define the condition clearly in adult, non-infantilizing language. Describe affected physiological systems, primary diagnostic criteria in current clinics, prevalence, inheritance mode, and the primary unmet clinical need.

Tier 2: Monogenic Architecture & DNA Sequence Alterations Genomics (SNVs/Indels)

Identify primary causal or susceptibility genes (HGNC symbol, Ensembl ID). Detail specific molecular variant classes. Report at least one authentic clinical variant from ClinVar with exact HGVS coordinates (c. and p.), Variation ID (VCV...), and GRCh38 coordinates. Explain its biochemical effect on the protein.

Tier 3: Cytogenetic & Structural Genomic Variants Structural / CNVs

Examine chromosomal rearrangements, copy number variants (CNVs), or segmental duplications (e.g. NAHR mechanisms). Compare clinical detection modalities (Karyotype vs. FISH vs. CMA vs. NGS discordant read mapping). (If purely monogenic SNV, explain structural modifier loci or why structural assays return negative results).

Tier 4: Statistical Genomics, GWAS & Polygenic Architecture GWAS • PRS • Equity

Identify top genome-wide significant associations (p < 5 × 10−8) from the GWAS Catalog (rsID, risk allele, Odds Ratio/Beta, p-value). Look up PRS metrics in the PGS Catalog. Conduct a mandatory Multi-Ancestry Equity Audit: evaluate the ancestry breakdown of the discovery cohort and explain the multi-ancestry portability trap when applied to diverse cohorts like Temple Health.

Tier 5: The Regulatory Genome: Epigenomics & 3D Chromatin Epigenome • TADs

Describe chromatin conformation at the locus. Identify ENCODE histone marks (H3K27ac active enhancers, H3K4me3 promoters) harboring non-coding risk variants. Discuss Topologically Associating Domains (TADs), CTCF insulation boundaries, or DNA methylation (5mC/imprinting/epigenetic clocks).

Tier 6: Functional Transcriptomics & Tissue Regulation (GTEx) GTEx • eQTLs • scRNA

Report tissue-specific expression from the GTEx Portal in Transcripts Per Million (TPM). Evaluate whether the disease-associated variant acts as a cis-eQTL in the relevant target tissue (report exact Normalized Effect Size [NES] and p-value). Summarize single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq) findings regarding cell-type specificity.

Tier 7: Metagenomics & Microbial / Environmental Influences Microbiome • Environment

Detail host-microbiome interactions, gut dysbiosis, bacterial metabolites (short-chain fatty acids [SCFAs], TMAO, bile acids), or viral/environmental triggers that modulate disease penetrance. (If non-microbiome, analyze host gene-by-environment risk interactions).

Tier 8: Precision Therapeutic Countermeasures & Translation Targeted Rx • Clinical Trials

Contrast conventional therapies with modern molecular countermeasures: targeted small molecules, splice-switching antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs), gene replacement, or CRISPR-Cas9 genome editing (*Casgevy* model). Benchmark an active clinical trial from ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT number, phase, endpoint). (Honors students must expand this section with a critical appraisal of surrogate trial endpoints, CPIC pharmacogenomic dosing guidelines, and socio-economic access barriers).

Tier 9 (Honors Requirement): Epistatic & Modifier Architecture Honors • BIOL 5111

Identify an authentic secondary genetic modifier or epistatic interactor gene (e.g. BCL11A modifying HBB in Sickle Cell; TREM2 modifying APOE in Alzheimer's; PCSK9 modifying LDLR; SLC26A9 modifying CFTR; ATG16L1 interacting with NOD2). Model the biochemical or regulatory interaction between both loci and explain how variation at the modifier alters clinical penetrance, age of onset, or therapeutic responsiveness.

5. Anti-Hallucination Guardrails: Mandatory Database Figures

📸 Mandatory Figure Specifications
  • Figure 1 (UCSC/Ensembl): Chromosome band, RefSeq/GENCODE gene model (exons/introns), and ≥1 ENCODE regulatory track (H3K27ac / DNase / CTCF). Must include student callout arrow pointing to the pathogenic exon or promoter.
  • Figure 2 (GTEx): Multi-tissue TPM expression violin plot OR cis-eQTL plot in affected tissue. Caption must state exact median TPM, NES, p-value, and sample size N.
  • Figure 3 (GWAS Catalog / PGS Catalog): Association plot/table for lead SNP + Ancestry Diversity Breakdown. Must annotate the non-European representation percentage and critique multi-ancestry portability.
  • Figure 4 (Honors / Graduate Requirement): Option A: 3D Macromolecular Structural Model (AlphaFold / RCSB PDB / PyMOL) mapping the pathogenic amino acid alteration onto functional active sites, binding clefts, or multimeric interfaces; OR
    Option B: Single-Cell or Spatial Transcriptomics Deconvolution (Human Cell Atlas / Single Cell Portal) demonstrating cell-type-specific pathology.
