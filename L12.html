Pharmacogenomics: Personalized Prescribing & Avoiding Fatal Dosing

Why the same standard tablet cures one patient, fails a second, and hospitalizes a third.
Slide 01
💊 The One-Size-Fits-All Prescribing Crisis
  • Pharmacogenomics (PGx), the study of how inherited genomic variation alters drug response and toxicity, replaces empirical trial-and-error prescribing.
  • Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs), harmful and unintended responses to medications given at standard doses, cause 2.2 million serious hospitalizations and >100,000 deaths/year in the US.
  • Ubiquitous Actionable Variants: Over 93% of humans carry at least one inherited variant that requires a dose change or drug switch across common prescriptions.

Foundations of Drug Response • PK vs. PD

Pharmacokinetics (PK) vs. Pharmacodynamics (PD)

Every genetic drug effect alters either how the body processes the drug or how the drug acts on its target.
Slide 02
⚖️ Two Independent Axes of Drug Action
  • Pharmacokinetics (PK), the quantitative study of how the body absorbs, distributes, metabolizes, and excretes a drug, determines drug concentration in blood over time.
  • Pharmacodynamics (PD), the biological effect a drug exerts on its target receptor, ion channel, or signaling pathway, governs tissue response at a given concentration.
  • Dual-Axis Precision: Some drugs—most famously warfarin—require testing both a PK enzyme gene (CYP2C9) and a PD target gene (VKORC1) simultaneously.
Memory Rule: PK = What the body does to the drug (concentration); PD = What the drug does to the body (receptor sensitivity).
Pharmacokinetics (PK) vs. Pharmacodynamics (PD) Two Axes of PGx
Ref: Roden et al. (Lancet 2019; 394:521–532) BIOL 3111 • PHARMACOGENOMICS
Pharmacokinetic Architecture • The ADME Pipeline

The Four Stages of ADME: From Pill to Clearance

How influx transporters, efflux pumps, hepatic enzymes, and renal tubules govern systemic drug exposure.
Slide 03
🔄 Tracing an Oral Medication Through the Body
  • Absorption & Distribution (A & D): Intestinal efflux pumps like P-glycoprotein (ABCB1), an ATP-driven membrane pump that ejects drugs back into the gut lumen, and hepatic uptake transporters like SLCO1B1 control how much drug enters the blood and liver.
  • Metabolism (M): Liver hepatocytes chemically modify lipophilic (fat-soluble) drugs via Phase I oxidation and Phase II conjugation.
  • Excretion (E): Hydrophilic (water-soluble) metabolites exit the body through renal tubular filtration in urine or biliary transport into feces.
Biophysical Reason for Metabolism: Unmodified fat-soluble drugs diffuse back across renal membranes—so the liver must make them water-soluble to be excreted!
The Four-Stage ADME Pharmacokinetic Pipeline A -> D -> M -> E
Ref: Giacomini et al. / International Transporter Consortium (Nat Rev Drug Discov 2010) BIOL 3111 • PHARMACOGENOMICS
Pharmacokinetic Architecture • Dosing Safety Margins

The Therapeutic Window & Therapeutic Index (TI)

Why pharmacogenomic testing is mandatory for narrow-therapeutic-index drugs where a 2-fold concentration shift is lethal.
Slide 04
📐 Measuring a Drug's Margin of Safety
  • Therapeutic Window, the plasma concentration range between the Minimum Effective Concentration (MEC) and the Minimum Toxic Concentration (MTC), defines safe dosing.
  • Therapeutic Index (TI = TD50 / ED50), the ratio of the dose causing toxicity in 50% of patients to the dose producing efficacy in 50%, quantifies safety margin.
  • Narrow Therapeutic Index (NTI, TI < 2–3): For drugs like warfarin, 5-fluorouracil, 6-mercaptopurine, and tacrolimus, losing one metabolizing allele pushes plasma levels above MTC.
Clinical Rule: Wide-window drugs (e.g., amoxicillin) tolerate 5× PK variation safely; narrow-window drugs turn 2× PK variation into organ failure.
Plasma Concentration–Time Curves Across Metabolizer Genotypes TI = TD50 / ED50
Ref: FDA Guidance on Narrow Therapeutic Index (NTI) Drugs; CPIC Guidelines BIOL 3111 • PHARMACOGENOMICS
Biophysical Foundations • Why the Body Must Process Medications

Why Do Drugs Need Chemical Processing at All?

Why we cannot simply inject every drug into the blood—and why fat-soluble pills would stay in the body forever without liver enzymes.
Slide 05
🧪 The Lipophilicity Catch-22: Entering vs. Leaving the Body
  • Why Not Deliver Every Drug Directly to Blood (IV)? Intravenous infusion requires sterile needles and clinic visits—impractical for millions of patients taking daily pills at home for blood pressure, depression, or clotting.
  • Getting In Requires Fat Solubility: To cross the fatty lipid bilayer of intestinal cells (and reach intracellular targets), oral drugs must be designed to be lipophilic, meaning fat-soluble and non-polar.
  • Getting Out Requires Water Solubility: When kidneys filter blood, fat-soluble drugs passively diffuse right back across renal tubule membranes into the blood—so the liver must convert them into hydrophilic (water-soluble) molecules to exit in urine!
Core Biophysical Rule: A drug must be fat-soluble to be absorbed from a pill into the blood, but must be made water-soluble by the liver so the kidneys can flush it out!
The Lipophilicity Catch-22: Gut Absorption vs. Kidney Elimination Fat-Soluble -> Water-Soluble
Ref: Lipinski et al. (Adv Drug Deliv Rev 2001); Benet et al. (Pharm Res 2011) BIOL 3111 • PHARMACOGENOMICS
Drug Delivery Architecture • Routes, Formulations & First-Pass Extraction

Route of Delivery, Pill Packaging & the First-Pass Effect

How swallowing a pill versus injecting IV or wearing a patch changes whether the liver intercepts the drug first.
Slide 06
📦 How Delivery Route and Packaging Shape Drug Fate
  • Oral Route & The Portal Vein Checkpoint: Everything absorbed from the stomach and intestine drains into the hepatic portal vein and passes straight through the liver before reaching the heart—called first-pass metabolism.
  • Direct-to-Blood Routes (IV, Sublingual, Patch, Inhaled): Injections, under-the-tongue tablets (nitroglycerin), and skin patches enter systemic veins directly—bypassing gut and first-pass liver destruction on Pass 1!
  • Why Pill Packaging Matters: Enteric coatings shield acid-fragile drugs from stomach HCl, extended-release matrices prevent toxic peak spikes, and prodrug packaging uses first-pass liver enzymes to unwrap the active drug.
Clinical Insight: Oral drugs with heavy first-pass extraction (codeine, clopidogrel, simvastatin, oral morphine) are vastly more sensitive to patient CYP genotype than IV drugs!
Oral Portal First-Pass Route vs. Direct Systemic Delivery & Packaging Route & Packaging

Hepatic Biotransformation • Phase I vs. Phase II Enzymes

Two-Step Liver Processing: Phase I vs. Phase II

How liver cells first attach a small polar hook (Phase I) and then bolt on a large water-soluble tag (Phase II).
Slide 07
🔧 The Two-Step Chemical Assembly Line in Liver Cells
  • Phase I — “Install a Small Polar Hook” (CYP450, DPYD): Enzymes perform a small chemical edit (adding an oxygen or cutting an ester bond) to expose a reactive handle (-OH, -NH2, -COOH). Phase I can either unwrap a prodrug or deactivate an active drug.
  • Phase II — “Bolt On a Water-Soluble Float” (UGT1A1, TPMT, NAT2): Transferase enzymes attach a large, ionized water-soluble molecule—such as a glucuronide sugar, sulfate, or methyl group—onto the drug.
  • Ready for Exit: Once tagged in Phase II, the bulky water-soluble conjugate cannot diffuse back across membranes and is flushed out in urine or bile.
Intuitive Rule: Phase I makes a small chemical edit (turning prodrugs ON or active drugs OFF); Phase II attaches a giant water-soluble tag for rapid disposal.
Inside the Hepatocyte: Phase I Hook -> Phase II Water-Soluble Tag Phase I & Phase II
Ref: Guengerich (Chem Res Toxicol 2008); Evans & Relling (Science 1999) BIOL 3111 • PHARMACOGENOMICS
Hepatic Biotransformation • The CYP450 Superfamily

The Cytochrome P450 (CYP450) Enzyme Superfamily

Why CYP2D6 makes up only 2% of liver CYP protein yet metabolizes 25% of all clinical prescriptions.
Slide 08
🧬 Decoding CYP450 Nomenclature & Workload
  • Decoding the Gene Symbol (e.g., CYP2D6): CYP = Cytochrome P450 heme-thiolate monooxygenase; 2 = Family (>40% amino acid identity); D = Subfamily (>55% identity); 6 = Specific isoform gene.
  • Six Isoforms Clear ~80% of Drugs: Out of 57 human CYP genes, just six—CYP3A4/5, CYP2D6, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP1A2, and CYP2B6—clear the vast majority of small-molecule medicines.
  • The CYP2D6 Disproportion: CYP2D6 accounts for only ~2% of hepatic CYP content, yet it metabolizes 20–25% of all prescribed drugs (opioids, SSRIs, TCAs, β-blockers, tamoxifen) and is highly polymorphic.
Clinical Focus: CYP3A4 clears the most drugs (~30–40%) but has rare loss-of-function variants; CYP2D6, CYP2C19, and CYP2C9 drive most polymorphic dosing errors!
Hepatic CYP450 Abundance vs. Prescription Clearance Share 57 Human CYP Genes

Historical Foundations • Birth of Pharmacogenetics

Historical Foundations: From Fava Beans to Modern CPIC

How primaquine hemolysis, succinylcholine paralysis, and isoniazid neuropathy revealed inherited enzyme defects.
Slide 09
🏛️ Four Milestones That Founded the Field
  • G6PD Deficiency (510 BCE → 1956): Pythagoras warned initiates against fava beans; in 1956 Carson & Alving showed that antimalarial primaquine causes red-cell hemolysis in soldiers lacking glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD).
  • Succinylcholine Apnea (Kalow, 1957): Patients who remained paralyzed for hours after surgical muscle relaxant succinylcholine carried an inherited defect in plasma butyrylcholinesterase (BChE).
  • Coined & Standardized (1957–2009): Motulsky (1957) and Vogel (1959) established pharmacogenetics; PharmGKB (2000) and CPIC (2009) built standardized clinical dosing rules.
Terminology: Pharmacogenetics studies single-gene drug reactions (e.g., BChE); Pharmacogenomics profiles variants across the whole genome.
Timeline of Pharmacogenetics & Clinical Standardization 510 BCE to 2026

Star Alleles & Diplotypes • PharmVar Standard

Decoding Star-Allele (*) Nomenclature

Why pharmacogenomics defines inherited haplotypes as numbered star alleles (*1, *2, *3, *17) rather than isolated SNPs.
Slide 10
⭐ What a Star Allele (*) Represents
  • Star Allele (*), a standardized label assigned by PharmVar to a specific haplotype (the combination of sequence variants inherited together on one chromosome copy), defines functional enzyme status.
  • The Reference Rule (*1): Across all pharmacogenes, *1 designates the wild-type reference allele with 100% normal enzyme activity.
  • Classic CYP2C19 Star Alleles: *2 (rs4244285, aberrant splice site) and *3 (rs4986893, premature stop codon) yield zero activity; *17 (rs12248560, promoter gain-of-function) boosts transcription.
Why Haplotypes Matter: Because humans are diploid, every patient has a diplotype (two star alleles, e.g., CYP2C19 *1/*2 or *2/*17).
Anatomy of Four Canonical CYP2C19 Star Alleles (chr10q23.33) PharmVar Standard

Star Alleles & Diplotypes • Structural Variation at chr22q13.2

Structural Copy Number Variants (CNVs) in CYP2D6

How flanking pseudogenes CYP2D7 and CYP2D8 drive whole-gene deletions (*5), tandem duplications (*2xN), and hybrid genes.
Slide 11
🧬 Unequal Crossover at the CYP2D6 Locus
  • Paralogous Pseudogene Trap (chr22q13.2): Active CYP2D6 sits immediately downstream of two nonfunctional pseudogenes, CYP2D7 and CYP2D8, that share 94–98% sequence homology.
  • Whole-Gene Deletion (CYP2D6*5): Unequal homologous recombination during meiosis deletes the entire active CYP2D6 coding sequence, yielding an Activity Value of 0.0.
  • Tandem Gene Duplication (*1x2, *2xN): The reciprocal crossover duplicates functional CYP2D6 up to N = 13 copies on one chromosome—creating Ultrarapid Metabolizers (UM).
Diagnostic Rule: Standard SNP arrays cannot count gene copies correctly without dedicated copy-number (CNV) probes—and duplicating a dead allele (*4x2) still gives 2 × 0 = 0 activity!
Chromosome 22q13.2 Structural Rearrangements of CYP2D6 *5 Deletion vs *2xN

Star Alleles & Diplotypes • CPIC Activity Score Math

From Diplotype to Phenotype: The Activity Score (AS)

How CPIC sums maternal and paternal allele values to classify patients into five clinical metabolizer phenotypes.
Slide 12
🧮 Calculating the CPIC Activity Score (AS)
  • Allele Activity Value: Each star allele receives a numeric functional weight: No Function = 0.0 (*4, *5), Decreased Function = 0.25 or 0.5 (*10, *41), and Normal Function = 1.0 (*1, *2).
  • The Diplotype Sum Rule: A patient's total Activity Score (AS) is the sum of their maternal and paternal alleles: AS = Value(Allele1) + Value(Allele2)
  • Five CPIC Phenotypes: Poor (PM, AS = 0), Intermediate (IM, 0.25–1.0), Normal (NM, 1.25–2.25), Rapid (RM), and Ultrarapid (UM, AS > 2.25).
Worked Example (CYP2D6): A patient with *1x2 / *4 has AS = (2 × 1.0) + 0.0 = 2.0 (Normal Metabolizer), whereas *1x2 / *1 has AS = 2.0 + 1.0 = 3.0 (Ultrarapid Metabolizer)!
CPIC Standardized CYP2D6 Activity Score & Phenotype Scale AS = Allele 1 + Allele 2
Ref: Caudle et al. / CPIC-DPWG Consensus CYP2D6 Scale (Clin Transl Sci 2020) BIOL 3111 • PHARMACOGENOMICS
Core Mechanistic Logic • Active Parent Drugs (Part 1)

The Central Rule of PGx (Part 1): Active Parent Drugs

When the swallowed pill is already pharmacologically active, hepatic metabolism turns the drug OFF.
Slide 13
🛑 Enzyme Inactivates an Active Parent Drug
  • Active Parent Drug, a medication whose swallowed chemical form directly binds its biological target without requiring enzymatic activation, relies on hepatic enzymes for clearance and inactivation.
  • Poor Metabolizer (PM, AS = 0): Because the clearance drain is blocked, active parent drug accumulates 5–10× in plasma—causing life-threatening overdose toxicity.
  • Ultrarapid Metabolizer (UM, AS > 2.25): The liver destroys the active drug before it reaches target tissues—causing subtherapeutic treatment failure.
Active Parent Drug Examples: Warfarin (CYP2C9), 5-Fluorouracil (DPYD), Phenytoin (CYP2C9), Tricyclic Antidepressants (CYP2D6), and SSRIs (CYP2C19).
Fate of an Active Parent Drug Across PM vs. UM Phenotypes Active Drug -> Inactive
Ref: Relling & Evans (Nature 2015; 526:343–350) BIOL 3111 • PHARMACOGENOMICS
Core Mechanistic Logic • Inactive Prodrugs (Part 2)

The Central Rule of PGx (Part 2): Inactive Prodrugs

When the swallowed pill is an inactive prodrug, hepatic metabolism turns the drug ON—completely inverting clinical risk!
Slide 14
🔄 Prodrug Bioactivation Flips PM and UM Risks
  • Prodrug, a pharmacologically inactive compound that must be converted by host enzymes into an active metabolite inside the body, relies on hepatic CYP enzymes to switch the drug ON.
  • Poor Metabolizer (PM, AS = 0): Cannot convert the inactive prodrug into the active molecule—resulting in zero therapeutic effect (Treatment Failure).
  • Ultrarapid Metabolizer (UM, AS > 2.25): Converts a massive surge of prodrug into active metabolite all at once—causing acute life-threatening toxicity!
Three High-Stakes Prodrugs: Clopidogrel (CYP2C19 → active thiol), Codeine / Tramadol (CYP2D6 → morphine), and Tamoxifen (CYP2D6 → endoxifen).
The Prodrug Inversion Matrix: PM Fails While UM Overdoses! Prodrug -> Active Drug
Ref: Relling & Evans (Nature 2015); CPIC Codeine & Clopidogrel Guidelines BIOL 3111 • PHARMACOGENOMICS
Core Mechanistic Logic • Drug–Drug–Gene Interactions

Phenoconversion: When Co-Medications Override DNA

How a potent CYP inhibitor converts a genotypic Normal Metabolizer (*1/*1) into a phenotypic Poor Metabolizer.
Slide 15
💊 Genotype Alone Is Not Enough Without the Med List
  • Phenoconversion, a mismatch between a patient's genetic metabolizer prediction and their actual in vivo enzyme capacity caused by co-administered drugs or inflammation, is a frequent clinical trap.
  • Strong CYP2D6 Inhibition (Zeroes Activity): Potent inhibitors like paroxetine, fluoxetine, or bupropion block >80% of CYP2D6 activity—multiplying the patient's Activity Score by 0.0.
  • Strong CYP2C19 Inhibition (Omeprazole): Co-prescribing proton-pump inhibitor omeprazole inhibits CYP2C19, blocking clopidogrel activation even in a *1/*1 patient!
CPIC Phenoconversion Math: Multiply the inhibited enzyme's Activity Score by 0.0 for a strong inhibitor (e.g., paroxetine) or by 0.5 for a moderate inhibitor (e.g., duloxetine).
Phenoconversion in Action: Tamoxifen + Paroxetine Interaction Drug-Drug-Gene Trap

Clinical Case 1 • CYP2D6 Prodrug Bioactivation

Case 1: CYP2D6 & Codeine vs. Tamoxifen

How CYP2D6 copy-number duplications turn pediatric codeine into a fatal morphine overdose while *4/*4 causes tamoxifen failure.
Slide 16
⚠️ Two Prodrugs Governed by CYP2D6
  • Codeine O-Demethylation: Codeine is an inactive opioid prodrug with 200× lower μ-opioid receptor affinity than morphine; hepatic CYP2D6 converts ~10% of codeine into active morphine.
  • The Pediatric & Breastfeeding Tragedy (UM): In CYP2D6 Ultrarapid Metabolizers (*1/*2xN, AS > 2.25), standard codeine syrup after tonsillectomy—or breast milk from a UM mother—causes fatal morphine respiratory arrest.
  • Tamoxifen in ER+ Breast Cancer (PM): In CYP2D6 *4/*4 Poor Metabolizers, impaired conversion to endoxifen leads CPIC to recommend switching to an aromatase inhibitor (anastrozole).
CPIC & FDA Boxed Warning: Avoid codeine and tramadol in both CYP2D6 Ultrarapid Metabolizers (overdose) and Poor Metabolizers (no analgesia)—use non-CYP2D6 analgesics (morphine, ketorolac)!

Cardiovascular Pharmacogenomics • Antiplatelet Therapy (Part 1)

Case 2 (Part 1): Clopidogrel Bioactivation by CYP2C19

Why 85% of absorbed clopidogrel is destroyed by esterases while the remaining 15% requires two CYP2C19 oxidation steps.
Slide 18
🩸 How Clopidogrel (Plavix) Prevents Blood Clots
  • Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT): After a heart attack or coronary artery stent placement (PCI), patients take aspirin plus a P2Y12 receptor blocker to stop platelets from clotting inside the metal stent.
  • The 85% Esterase Sink: Clopidogrel is an oral thienopyridine prodrug; hepatic carboxylesterase-1 (CES1) immediately hydrolyzes 85% of the absorbed dose into an inactive carboxylic acid.
  • Two-Step CYP2C19 Bioactivation: The surviving 15% undergoes two sequential hepatic CYP2C19 oxidations (via 2-oxo-clopidogrel) to generate the active thiol metabolite that irreversibly blocks platelet P2Y12.
Biochemical Vulnerability: Because 85% of clopidogrel is already lost to CES1, any loss-of-function mutation in CYP2C19 collapses active thiol production!
Hepatic Competing Pathways of Clopidogrel Bioactivation 85% CES1 vs 15% CYP2C19

Case 2 (Part 2): CYP2C19*2/*3 & Coronary Stent Thrombosis

Why the FDA placed a Boxed Warning on clopidogrel and why CPIC switches IM/PM patients to Prasugrel or Ticagrelor.
Slide 19
🫀 Clinical Consequence & CPIC Therapeutic Switch
  • 3-Fold Stent Thrombosis Surge: In patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), carrying one (IM: *1/*2, *2/*17) or two (PM: *2/*2, *2/*3) loss-of-function alleles triples stent thrombosis risk (HR ≈ 3.09, Mega et al. NEJM 2009).
  • Why *2/*17 Is Still an Intermediate Metabolizer: Even when paired with a gain-of-function *17 allele on the other chromosome, one null *2 allele leaves platelet inhibition dangerously incomplete.
  • The CPIC & FDA Action: Switch CYP2C19 IM and PM patients to Prasugrel (Effient) or Ticagrelor (Brilinta), which do not depend on CYP2C19.
Why Ticagrelor Works: Ticagrelor is an active parent drug (not a prodrug) that binds P2Y12 directly without requiring hepatic CYP2C19 bioactivation!

Cardiovascular Pharmacogenomics • Warfarin Anticoagulation (Part 1)

Case 3 (Part 1): Warfarin's Dual PK/PD Axis (CYP2C9 + VKORC1)

Why daily warfarin requirements vary 20-fold (0.5 mg to 10+ mg/day) across patients.
Slide 21
🩸 Combining PK Clearance with PD Target Sensitivity
  • PK Clearance Gate (CYP2C9): Warfarin is an active anticoagulant given as an R/S racemic mixture; hepatic CYP2C9 clears the 3–5× more potent S-warfarin. Missense variants *2 (p.Arg144Cys, ~70% loss) and *3 (p.Ile359Leu, ~90% loss) trap S-warfarin in blood.
  • PD Target Gate (VKORC1): Warfarin inhibits Vitamin K Epoxide Reductase Complex 1 (VKORC1), blocking clotting factors II, VII, IX, and X.
  • Promoter Variant VKORC1 -1639G>A (rs9923231): The A allele cuts hepatic VKORC1 mRNA expression by ~50%—so half as much target enzyme requires half as much warfarin!
Additive Risk: A patient with CYP2C9 *3/*3 (slow clearance) AND VKORC1 AA (low target) needs only 0.5–1.5 mg/day instead of 5 mg/day—standard dosing causes fatal brain hemorrhage!

Case 3 (Part 2): Why the 2013 COAG Warfarin Trial Failed

How testing only European CYP2C9 *2/*3 alleles caused an algorithm to misclassify and overdose African-American patients.
Slide 22
⚖️ The Tale of Two 2013 NEJM Warfarin Trials
  • EU-PACT Succeeded (Pirmohamed NEJM 2013): In a 98.5% European cohort, genotype-guided warfarin dosing significantly increased time in therapeutic INR range (67.4% vs. 60.3%, p < 0.001).
  • COAG Failed & Harmed Black Patients (Kimmel NEJM 2013): In a diverse US cohort (27% African American), the genotyping panel only tested European CYP2C9*2 and *3—omitting African-enriched loss-of-function alleles *5, *6, *8, *11 and rs12777823!
  • The Default-to-*1/*1 Error: Black patients carrying CYP2C9*8 were falsely labeled *1/*1 (Normal) and given high warfarin doses—causing excess Supratherapeutic INR bleeding!
CPIC 2017 Mandate: Never use a CYP2C9 warfarin algorithm in African-ancestry patients unless the panel explicitly genotypes *5, *6, *8, *11 and rs12777823!

Case 4: SLCO1B1*5 (rs4149056) & Statin Myopathy

How a single missense variant in hepatic uptake transporter OATP1B1 traps simvastatin in skeletal muscle.
Slide 23
💪 Transporter Failure: Trapping Statins Outside the Liver
  • Normal Statin Trafficking (SLCO1B1): Statins inhibit cholesterol synthesis inside hepatocytes. The liver uptake transporter OATP1B1 (encoded by SLCO1B1 on chr12p12.1) pulls statin acid from portal blood into the liver.
  • The SLCO1B1*5 Missense Variant (rs4149056, c.521T>C, p.Val174Ala): Disrupts OATP1B1 membrane localization, blocking hepatic uptake so statin plasma exposure (AUC) jumps 221%.
  • The SEARCH GWAS Discovery (NEJM 2008): Each C allele increases simvastatin 80 mg myopathy risk by OR = 4.5—and OR = 16.9 in CC homozygotes!
CPIC Statin Action: In SLCO1B1 Decreased/Poor Function patients, avoid high-dose simvastatin—switch to rosuvastatin or pravastatin (less dependent on OATP1B1) or cap dose at ≤20 mg!
